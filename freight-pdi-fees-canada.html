Can You Negotiate Freight and PDI Fees in Canada?

The short answer, what these fees actually are, and how to still lower your total price.

Written by the CarBrokerCanada team.

The Short Answer

Mostly no, but effectively yes. You almost certainly cannot get a dealer to remove or reduce the freight (destination) or PDI (pre-delivery inspection) charge itself. Those amounts are set by the manufacturer, printed on the invoice, and applied to every unit of that model that arrives in Canada, so the dealer has no real authority to waive them. Asking a salesperson to "take off freight" is a common instinct, but it puts the negotiation in the wrong place from the start.

What you can do is negotiate an equivalent discount somewhere else in the deal, whether that's the purchase price, accessories, or the first few payments, so the total out-the-door cost drops by roughly the same amount. The fee stays on the invoice, but your net cost still goes down. In other words, the line item survives, but its impact on your wallet does not have to.

What Freight and PDI Actually Are

Freight, sometimes called the destination or delivery charge, covers the cost of shipping the vehicle from the factory to the dealership. PDI covers the technician time spent inspecting, cleaning, and preparing the car before it's handed over to you. Both are charged by the manufacturer, not invented by the individual dealership, and both show up as separate line items on the manufacturer's invoice to that dealer.

In Canada, freight commonly runs somewhere in the range of $1,800 to $2,500 or more on mainstream new vehicles, with PDI typically bundled into that same combined line rather than billed on its own. The exact figure depends on the manufacturer, the model, and the model year, so treat any number here as a general guide rather than a fixed rate. Because the amount is standardized across every dealer selling that model, you'll typically see close to the same freight and PDI charge no matter which showroom you visit, which is part of why dealers rarely budge on it: they aren't the ones setting the price, and undercutting it would come straight out of their own margin rather than the manufacturer's.

Fees That Are Actually Worth Challenging

Freight and PDI sit in a different category from the fees dealers add on themselves. Admin or documentation fees vary widely from one dealership to the next for the exact same paperwork, which is a sign they have flexibility built in. Nitrogen-filled tires, window etching, and protection packages (fabric coating, rust proofing bundles, and similar add-ons) are frequently marked up well beyond their cost and can often be declined outright or negotiated down significantly. These are the charges a dealership actually invents, prices, and profits from, which is exactly why there's real room to push back on them. If you're going to spend your negotiating energy anywhere, spend it here rather than on the manufacturer-set freight and PDI line.

What to Say at the Dealership

Don't ask the dealer to remove freight or PDI outright; that request rarely goes anywhere and can make the rest of the conversation harder. Instead, ask for the out-the-door price, meaning the single number that includes the vehicle price, freight, PDI, and every other fee combined. Once you have that one number, negotiate against it as a whole rather than arguing over individual line items. This keeps the focus on what actually matters, which is the total amount leaving your bank account, and gives the dealer room to move on the parts of the deal they actually control.

A useful script sounds something like this: "What's my full out-the-door price, everything included?" Once they give you that number, respond with a lower target for that same total figure, and let the dealer decide internally whether that comes off the vehicle price, an add-on, or a rebate. You never have to mention freight or PDI by name, and you never need the dealer to admit they moved on a fee they normally hold firm on.
