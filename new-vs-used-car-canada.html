Should You Buy a New or Used Car in Canada?

There's no universal winner. Here's the framework that actually answers the question for your situation.

Written by the CarBrokerCanada team.

The Honest Answer

There is no universal winner between new and used in Canada. Anyone who tells you otherwise is skipping the parts that actually decide the answer. What matters is how long you plan to keep the vehicle, how tight the used market currently is for the specific model you want, and whether new-car financing rates are subsidized enough to close the price gap. Get those three factors right for your situation and the new-versus-used question mostly answers itself.

Depreciation Still Happens, But the Math Has Shifted

A new vehicle loses the largest share of its value in the first few years of ownership, and that hasn't changed. What has changed is the used side of the equation. Used vehicle prices in Canada have stayed historically elevated since the pandemic era, and on popular models that elevated pricing hasn't fully unwound. That narrows the classic used-car advantage: if a three-year-old version of the model you want is still selling for a large share of its original price, the traditional case for "let someone else eat the depreciation" gets weaker. On models with softer demand, the old logic still holds and used remains the clearly cheaper path.

The Financing Rate Flip

This is the factor buyers most often miss. Manufacturers frequently subsidize new-car financing rates well below what a bank or credit union will offer on a used-car loan. When that gap is wide enough, the extra interest cost on a used loan can eat up most, or all, of the sticker price difference between the new and used version of the same model over the life of the loan. Before you assume used is cheaper, run the actual numbers on both, purchase price and total financing cost together, not sticker price alone.

Certified Pre-Owned: The Middle Path

Certified pre-owned programs exist for exactly this dilemma. A CPO vehicle has been through a manufacturer-backed inspection, comes with an extended warranty, and is priced between a comparable private-sale used vehicle and a brand-new one. You give up some of the savings a private used purchase would offer, but you buy down a meaningful amount of repair risk and uncertainty. For buyers who want most of the savings of used without the full unknown of a private sale, CPO is often the most sensible middle ground.

Warranty and Repair Risk

A new vehicle comes with a full manufacturer warranty from day one, which caps your downside on major repairs for several years. A used vehicle, especially one bought privately outside of a CPO program, carries more repair uncertainty the older and higher-mileage it gets. That risk is hard to price precisely, but it's real, and it should factor into your comparison, particularly if you're the type of owner who wants predictable costs rather than the possibility of a large surprise repair bill.

Don't Forget the Tax

A common assumption is that used cars are tax-free. In most provinces, that's wrong: you pay sales tax on a used vehicle purchase whether it comes from a dealer or a private seller. The exact treatment for private sales varies by province, so confirm the rules where you live rather than assuming a used purchase saves you the full tax amount. It rarely does.

When New Clearly Wins

New is the clear choice when you plan to keep the vehicle 8 or more years (so depreciation gets amortized over a long ownership period), when the model you want has strong manufacturer incentives or a subsidized financing rate, or when the used market for that specific model is tight enough that used prices sit uncomfortably close to new.

When Used Clearly Wins

Used is the clear choice when you're looking at a vehicle roughly 3 to 5 years old with a model that has soft resale value, when you're paying cash and financing rate incentives don't apply to you, or when the steepest depreciation has already been absorbed by the first owner and you're buying past that drop.
