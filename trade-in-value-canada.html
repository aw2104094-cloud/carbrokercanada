How Do You Get the Most for Your Trade-In in Canada?

Get outside offers first, keep the trade-in separate from the new-car negotiation, and let the tax math work in your favour.

Written by the CarBrokerCanada team.

The Short Answer

The most for your trade-in comes from doing three things in order. First, get outside offers on your vehicle before you ever set foot in a dealership. Second, keep the trade-in conversation completely separate from the new-car price negotiation until that price is settled. Third, remember that a decent trade-in offer often beats a slightly higher private-sale price once you account for the sales-tax offset. Most people lose value by skipping straight to "what will you give me for my car" before they've locked in anything else.

Why the Tax Math Favours Trading In

In most provinces, when you trade in a vehicle as part of a new purchase, sales tax applies only to the difference between the new vehicle's price and your trade-in value, at your provincial rate. On a $10,000 trade-in, that can mean roughly $1,300 in tax savings at a 13% rate, money you simply don't get back if you sell the car privately. That offset doesn't show up in the sticker price comparison, which is exactly why so many buyers undervalue what a trade-in is actually worth to them on paper.

Get Real Numbers Before You Negotiate

Before you talk to a dealer about your trade-in, get real numbers from somewhere else. Online instant-offer services and an appraisal from a second dealership give you a floor value you can hold the first dealer to. Walking in with a competing number in hand changes the entire conversation. Instead of asking what your car is worth, you're asking a dealer to beat or match an offer you already have, which is a far stronger position than negotiating blind.

Watch for the Blended Number

The classic dealer play is blending the trade-in value, the new-car price, and the monthly payment into one figure so you can't tell where the value actually went. A generous-sounding trade-in offer can quietly disappear into a worse price on the new vehicle, and a low monthly payment can hide both. The counter is simple: settle the new-car price first, as its own negotiation, before the trade-in ever comes up. Only once that number is fixed should you discuss what they'll give you for your current vehicle.

When Private Sale Still Wins

Trading in isn't always the better move. High-demand models with strong private markets, like popular trucks and low-mileage vehicles in tight supply, often sell for meaningfully more to a private buyer. If you can tolerate the time, the listing photos, the test drives, and the back-and-forth with strangers, private sale usually grosses more. Be honest with yourself about what it nets, though. After the tax offset you give up and the hours you put in, the gap over a strong trade-in offer is often smaller than it looks on paper.
